## Why eBay first, and why that is not giving up on the site

Three channels, ranked by how long each takes to produce a pound.

### 1. eBay — weeks

The demand is already there. Somebody typing "reclaimed teak console table"
into eBay is a buyer, not a browser, and eBay does not care that kaikuhome.com
is nine months old with no backlinks. Authority is the thing Kaiku does not
have and eBay is the channel that does not require it.

The economics are unusually good here and worth restating because they were
wrong in this ledger for weeks: **a private seller pays 0% final value fee.**
The cost is 35p per listing past the free allowance. Not 13%, not 12.8%. On a
£260 D.I. Designs console table at 30% margin that is £78 of gross profit
against 35p of cost.

### 2. Google Shopping free listings — weeks to months

The feed works. It is throttled, and three specific things are suspected:

- the feed's target country, which is sending 85% of impressions to the
  Netherlands and Germany
- 136 items Merchant Center will not show, cause unknown
- 127 products flagged backorder because their stock status is blank

All three are settings or answers rather than engineering. The engineering
half — brand/GTIN, descriptions, country signals — is done and live.

### 3. Organic search — six to twelve months, and not before

Position 37 to page one, on a nine-month-old domain with no external links, is
not a quarter's work. Saying otherwise would be the fifth wrong ranking claim
on this project. **The website will not be the first channel to make money and
planning as if it will is what has made this feel hopeless.**

---

## What the website is actually for, in the meantime

Not nothing. It is the thing that makes the other two work.

- An eBay listing from a seller with 0 feedback and no shop behind it converts
  worse than one from a seller with a real website carrying the same products,
  the same photographs and a returns policy. The site is the credibility
  behind the listing.
- Google Shopping listings _are_ the website. Every free listing points at a
  product page. The feed work of the last fortnight is spent on this channel,
  not on organic rankings.
- Repeat customers and email belong to Kaiku, not to eBay. eBay rents you a
  buyer once. The site keeps them.

So the site is infrastructure for the first year, not a shopfront waiting for
footfall. That reframing is the difference between "nobody is visiting my
website" and "my website is doing its job while another channel brings the
money".

## The sequence, one thing at a time

**Now → 8 weeks: money from eBay.**
List the permitted 194, starting with the ones nobody else is selling. The
scarcity ranking is already done in `docs/ebay-scarce-products.md` and the
field-by-field listing content in `docs/ebay-first-eight.md`. This is the only
phase that produces cash, and cash is what makes everything after it possible.

**In parallel, twenty minutes total: unthrottle Shopping.**
The three settings above. They need one person once, not a project.

**8 weeks → 6 months: the things that compound.**
Feedback on eBay. Reviews on the site — Google Customer Reviews is installed
and cannot seed itself without orders, so it starts the day the first eBay
customer is asked. Supplier range widened to the ones nobody else carries.
Premier Housewares asked for marketplace permission, which is 546 more
products.

**6 months onwards: authority, and only then organic.**
Links, press, brand searches. `docs/link-outreach.md` and
`docs/press-pitch.md` are written and have been sent to nobody. This is the
slowest lever and the only one that makes organic search work, which is why it
should be started early and expected late.

## The honest constraint nobody has written down

Damien has said three true things that no plan can ignore: he is short of
time, he has no money to buy stock, and he is tired of it.

- **Time.** One channel at a time. eBay first. Not eBay and content and
  outreach and suppliers in the same week.
- **Money.** Dropshipping does not need stock. The customer pays, then the
  supplier is paid from that money. What it needs is an open trade account, and
  Hill Interiors and D.I. Designs are both open. The cash constraint is real
  but it does not block the 194.
- **Tiredness.** The listing work is the boring, repetitive, unavoidable part,
  and it is the part that pays. Everything else on this project is easier and
  worth less.
