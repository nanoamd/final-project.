# Local press — the pitch, and what to say

The angle is Damien, not Kaiku. Local papers run people. "Bourne End shop
launches website" does not get printed; a named 20-year-old who spent years
trying and has finally started does.

Everything below has gaps marked `[FILL]`. **They are the parts that make it
run.** A journalist can tell within one line whether a pitch is specific or
padded, and specificity is the entire difference between a reply and no reply.

---

## Two things to decide before sending anything

**1. This is permanent and it is about you.** Your name, your age and your
photograph become searchable and stay searchable. That is the trade you are
making for the coverage, and it is a real one — not a reason to avoid it, but a
reason to decide it on purpose rather than discover it later.

**2. Do not overstate the trading.** The site is live, the Merchant Centre feed
is live, eBay is live — and the first sale has not landed. If a journalist asks
how it is going and the answer dresses that up, it will be checked, and a
correction in a local paper is worse than no article.

The honest version is also the better story. "I have just launched and it is
terrifying" is a piece a local editor will run. "Successful young entrepreneur"
with no sales is a piece that falls apart on the first question. Lead with the
start, not with success you have not had yet.

## Who to send it to

| Title                     | Group                      | Why                                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bucks Free Press**      | Newsquest                  | Covers Bourne End and High Wycombe. Main target.                                               |
| **Maidenhead Advertiser** | Baylis Media (independent) | Covers Bourne End's side of the river. Independent titles link out more often than group ones. |
| Marlow Free Press         | Newsquest                  | Sister title, same newsroom — send to one or the other, not both.                              |
| Bucks Herald              | National World             | Wider county, weaker local hook.                                                               |

Find the news desk address on each site's Contact page and send to a named
reporter where you can — "newsdesk@" is a shared inbox, a named business or
community reporter is a person.

**On the link:** Newsquest titles generally apply `nofollow` to outbound
commercial links, which means no SEO value from the link itself. I could not
confirm that for these specific titles — their pages are JavaScript-rendered, so
it is the general pattern rather than something checked. Go in expecting the
mention rather than the link, and treat a followed link as a bonus.

## The pitch email

Short. A news desk decides in about four seconds.

> **Subject:** Bourne End 20-year-old launches home and garden shop after
> [FILL: N] failed attempts
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Damien, I'm 20 and I live in [FILL: Bourne End / nearby]. I've just
> launched Kaiku, an online home and garden shop — [FILL: N] products, built on
> my own over [FILL: how long].
>
> It's the [FILL: third/fourth] business I've tried to start since I was
> [FILL: age]. The others were [FILL: one line — what they were and what
> happened].
>
> The part I think your readers might find interesting: I've given away 17 free
> planning tools with no sign-up, because I got sick of furniture guides that
> tell you to buy a coffee table two-thirds the length of your sofa and never
> check whether the room has the floor for it. kaikuhome.com/tools
>
> Happy to talk, and happy to be photographed. I'm around [FILL: when].
>
> Damien [FILL: surname]
> [FILL: phone]
> kaikuhome.com

## What makes it run, and what kills it

**Makes it run:**

- **A number and an age together.** "20 years old, 900 products, built alone" is
  three concrete facts in seven words.
- **The failures, named.** This is the whole story. "I tried [FILL] at 16 and it
  made £[FILL]" is worth more than any description of the current site. Editors
  want the attempt that did not work.
- **A local peg.** Bourne End, named, early.
- **A photograph.** Say yes before you are asked. A story with a picture runs
  bigger than one without, and regional papers are short-staffed enough that
  "I'll come to you" is a genuine help.
- **Something they can try.** The tools are demonstrable in thirty seconds on a
  phone. A reporter who can use the thing writes a better piece.

**Kills it:**

- Vagueness. "Years of struggle" from a 20-year-old, with no detail, reads as
  padding. "Three attempts since I was 16, and here is what each one was" does
  not.
- Marketing language. Any sentence containing "passionate", "curated" or
  "journey" gets the email deleted.
- A press release attached to a first email. Send the short version; send the
  detail if they reply.
- Asking for a link. Never ask. A reporter who feels used stops replying, and
  the mention is worth more than the link anyway.

## The quotes to have ready

A journalist will ask for these. Have them written down, then say them in your
own words rather than reading them.

**On the failed attempts** — the one that matters most:

> "[FILL: I started trying to make something work when I was 16. The first one
> was ___ and it made about £___. The second was ___ and I gave up when ___.]
> I kept starting and stopping. This is the first one I have actually finished
> and put in front of people."

**On why this one is different:**

> "[FILL: what is actually different — you built the whole thing, you have real
> supplier accounts, you did not quit at the hard part.] It is the first time
> I have had something real enough to be embarrassed about."

**On the free tools:**

> "Every guide online tells you a coffee table should be two-thirds the length
> of your sofa. None of them ask whether you have the floor for it. So you get a
> table that is the right shape and blocks the way past the sofa. I built the
> tools I wanted and could not find, and I am not putting them behind an email
> address."

**On where it is now — the honest one:**

> "[FILL: honest status.] I am not going to pretend it is a success yet. It has
> been live for [FILL] and I am still waiting on the first proper order. But it
> exists, which is further than the last three got."

That last quote is the one that will get you the piece. Editors do not print
confidence; they print someone being straight.

---

## Have ready before you send

1. A phone number you will answer.
2. A photograph of yourself — at a desk, or with something you sell. Not a
   selfie, not a logo.
3. The three or four hard numbers: how many products, how many suppliers, how
   long it took, what it cost you.
4. Your availability for the next week.

---

## What to expect

One in three or four local papers reply to a pitch this specific — better odds
than any other tier in `docs/link-outreach.md`, because you are offering them
content rather than asking for a favour.

**What it is worth is not the clicks.** Expect 20 to 80 click-throughs over two
or three days and then nothing, and expect no sales from them — that is
curiosity traffic from neighbours, not shoppers.

What it is worth is the named brand mention, and the line "as featured in the
Bucks Free Press" in every blogger email you send afterwards. That second-order
effect is worth more than the traffic, and it is the reason to do it.
