# Where the catalogue does not look premium

From `scripts/audit-price-positioning.ts`, 2 September 2026. Thresholds are
judgements and live in constants at the top of that script: entry floor £25,
anchor target £250, thin below 6 products.

## The pattern worth naming first

**Breadth is concentrated in the cheap end.** The four biggest non-furniture
categories are the four with the lowest medians:

| Category             | Products | Median | Top  |
| -------------------- | -------- | ------ | ---- |
| Planters             | 86       | £60    | £246 |
| Wall Clocks          | 36       | £53    | £199 |
| Bathroom Accessories | 35       | £27    | £49  |
| Water Features       | 27       | £45    | £120 |

Those four are 184 products — a fifth of the catalogue — and a shopper browsing
the widest parts of the shop sees the cheapest things in it. Meanwhile the
furniture categories are genuinely well-positioned: Sofas £272–£2,078, TV Units
£139–£1,591, Console Tables £127–£957, Kitchen Furniture £129–£2,459.

So product count as the edge is sound, but right now count and cheapness are the
same axis. The fix is not fewer cheap products, it is **more expensive ones in
the categories that already have depth.**

## What to ask a new supplier for

In priority order, by what would change the shop most:

1. **Rugs.** An empty category with copy already written, high value, high
   margin, and it cross-sells against every furniture range we are already
   strong in. Nothing else on this list is as ready.
2. **Premium planters** — £250 to £800. The category has 86 products and depth
   already; it needs a ceiling. Large statement pots, aged stone, corten steel.
3. **Bathroom** across the board — lighting, towel rails, and accessories above
   £50. Three of the worst positioned entries are all in one room, so one good
   bathroom supplier fixes a whole department.
4. **Statement lighting for kitchen and office** — both thin at 5 and 4 products,
   in a room type where a single pendant or floor lamp carries a high price
   comfortably.
5. **Water features and garden lighting above £250.** Both cap out around £110–£120
   today; both are categories where £400–£1,500 pieces genuinely exist.
6. **Outdoor storage** — 4 products topping out at £149. Premium timber and
   powder-coated steel stores run to £600+.

## What NOT to add

More sub-£25 decor. The catalogue has enough, it is already the loudest part of
the shop by volume, and every additional cheap line makes the positioning problem
worse rather than the breadth advantage better.

Also worth knowing before signing anyone: on Hill's terms a £20 item carries
£6.99 of carriage and cannot be sold at market price at all when dropshipped
singly. Cheap products only work from a supplier whose carriage is included, or
bought to stock. **Ask about carriage terms before range.**
