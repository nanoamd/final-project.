# Suppliers and brands for the premium gaps

Researched 2 September 2026, against the gaps in

## First: you have no third-party brands at all

897 of 906 products are branded **Kaiku**. The only other brand with products is
SaunaPlunge, at 8.

Three brand records exist in Sanity with **zero products attached**:

| Brand                  | Products | What it is                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Harvia                 | 0        | The largest sauna and steam manufacturer in the world. Finnish, publicly listed.  |
| Auroom                 | 0        | Premium Latvian sauna cabins, the design end of the market.                       |
| Mercia Garden Products | 0        | UK timber garden buildings manufacturer, runs an active trade dropship programme. |

**That is the famous-brand partnership, already half set up.** Harvia in
particular sits in the highest-value department in the shop — Sauna runs
£2,813–£6,500 — and is the one name a serious sauna buyer already recognises and
searches for. Someone intended to stock all three and never did.

Two reasons this beats a cold approach to a lifestyle brand:

- **The category is already yours.** Sauna and Cold Plunge are the departments
  with real depth of copy, tools and buying guides. A Harvia range lands into a
  section built for it rather than needing one.
- **Search intent already exists.** Nobody types "Kaiku sauna". People type
  "Harvia sauna UK". An own-brand-only catalogue has no branded search volume at
  all, which is a structural ceiling on traffic no amount of product count fixes.

## On "famous brand partnership" generally

The premium names people think of first — Cox & Cox, Neptune, Garden Trading —
do not appear in any UK dropship or trade directory, and that is deliberate:
they are vertically integrated direct-to-consumer businesses. Their brand _is_
the margin, so they do not hand it to third-party retailers.

Where brand partnerships are actually available, it is **manufacturer** brands
rather than lifestyle retailers, and they come with conditions worth knowing
before you ask:

- **MAP or RRP enforcement.** You get the name and the credibility, not price
  freedom. Fixed margin, usually decent in pounds, and no discounting.
- **Brand approval of your site.** Which is now a much easier conversation than
  it was a week ago: 906 products with real descriptions, category buying guides
  and FAQs reads as a real retailer.
- **Minimum spend, often stocking rather than dropship** for the recognised names.

## Supplier shortlist by gap

### Rugs — the empty category, top priority

**Premier Housewares already sells rugs — 86 of them.** You have the account, the
VAT position is known, and no new onboarding is needed. That fills the empty
category this week. It will not give you premium anchors, because Premier is a
value supplier, but an empty category is a worse problem than a mid-priced one.

For the anchor end: The Real Rug Company (direct dispatch, whole range without
stocking), Think Rugs (trade accounts, live stock and automated ordering).

### Premium planters — 86 products with no ceiling

**Adezz** is the recognised premium planter brand — corten steel and fibreglass,
built for UK weather, exactly the £250–£800 anchor the category lacks. Available
through UK trade distributors rather than direct:

- **Riverhill Garden Supplies** — ADEZZ corten and fibreglass, extra-large pots,
  25 years trading, offers free warehousing to trade
- **Yard Garden Style** — Adezz UK supplier
- **Taylor Made Planters** — own corten and fibreglass ranges, **trade pricing
  with free UK mainland delivery**

That last term matters more than the price list. Carriage included is what makes
margin work: it is the single term whose absence cost £1,250 on Hill.

### Garden buildings and premium outdoor — Mercia

Already a brand record with no products, and they run a trade partner programme
for UK retailers with dropship, post-purchase support and marketing materials.
Timber garden buildings are a natural extension of Sauna and Outdoor Living, and
they are high-ticket.

**Zest Outdoor Living** is the comparable option — wooden garden furniture,
gazebos, arches and planters, dropshipped to the customer.

### Bathroom — the worst-positioned department

No strong lead yet. This is the gap most worth a dedicated search: lighting,
towel rails, and accessories above £50 all in one room, so one supplier fixes
three of the weakest entries in the whole catalogue.

## On Aosom

Damien is right that Aosom can add breadth. But look at where their listed
products sit today:

| Category             | Products | Range         | Median   |
| -------------------- | -------- | ------------- | -------- |
| Fire Pits & Heating  | 21       | £120–£249     | £186     |
| Lighting             | 15       | £39–£79       | £58      |
| Desks                | 12       | £42–£229      | £79      |
| **Garden Furniture** | 12       | **£387–£938** | **£590** |
| Garden Lighting      | 11       | £39–£109      | £45      |
| Privacy Screens      | 10       | £99–£119      | £109     |

Five of those six are categories flagged as having **no anchor above £250**, and
Aosom's stock in them tops out at £249. So adding more Aosom lines in those
categories makes the positioning problem worse, not better.

The exception is the one that matters: **Aosom's Garden Furniture is £387–£938**.
That is genuinely the premium end. So the instruction to Aosom is not "more
products", it is **more of their top of range** — and their carriage terms need
recording before any of it can be priced honestly, since 81 of their 103 current
products have no carriage figure at all.
