# Kaiku Blog SEO Playbook

How to take a Kaiku journal post from draft to genuinely rankable. This is the
exact checklist used for the first optimized post ("How Much Does an Outdoor
Sauna Cost in the UK?"), written to be repeated for every post after it.

Honest framing first: no checklist guarantees "thousands of views." What this
process does is target keywords real buyers type, structure the page the way
Google rewards, and compound — each post makes the next one stronger. Traffic
follows consistency, not any single post.

## Step 1 — Pick the keyword before writing a word

Write for **one primary keyword phrase with buying intent**, not a topic.

- Good: "outdoor sauna cost UK", "infrared vs traditional sauna", "barrel
  sauna vs cabin sauna", "cold plunge temperature guide", "sauna planning
  permission UK"
- Bad: "the joy of sauna culture" (nobody searches it), "best saunas 2026"
  (dominated by huge review sites you can't outrank yet)

Rules of thumb at Kaiku's current authority level:

- Prefer **question keywords** ("how much", "do I need", "what temperature") —
  lower competition, and they match how people actually type.
- Prefer **UK-specific angles** — planning permission, UK electricity prices,
  UK delivery. Big US sites can't compete on these, and your buyers are UK.
- One post = one keyword. Two keywords = two posts.

Free validation: type the phrase into Google and look at (a) the "People also
ask" box — those are your H2s, (b) who ranks — if page one is all Wickes/B&Q/
national press, pick a narrower phrase.

## Step 2 — Structure the page the way Google reads it

- **Title (and meta title)**: the keyword phrase, near the front, under 60
  characters. "How Much Does an Outdoor Sauna Cost in the UK? (2026)" — the
  year signals freshness and lifts clicks.
- **First paragraph answers the question directly.** Google pulls this as the
  snippet. No throat-clearing intro — the number, then the nuance.
- **H2s = the sub-questions** from "People also ask". Each H2 should be
  answerable standalone (that's what wins featured snippets).
- **An FAQ section at the end** with 3–5 real questions, one-paragraph
  answers.
- 1,200–2,000 words. Long enough to be thorough, never padded.

## Step 3 — Internal links (this is where a shop beats a blog)

Every post must link to:

- **The relevant category page** (/shop/outdoor-saunas) — at least twice,
  naturally ("our outdoor sauna collection").
- **2–3 specific products** with real prices — this is credibility no
  content-only site can match, and it moves readers into the funnel.
- **One other guide** (/learn/choosing-a-sauna) — topical authority is built
  by guides referencing each other.

And the reverse: when a new post goes live, add a link **to** it from the
category page or an older related post. Orphan posts don't rank.

## Step 4 — Metadata & structured data (already automated)

The site already renders canonical URLs, OpenGraph/Twitter cards, and
JSON-LD sitewide, and the sitemap updates automatically. Per post you only
need to fill in the Studio's SEO fields:

- **Meta title**: keyword-front, ≤60 chars (defaults to the post title).
- **Meta description**: 140–155 chars, includes the keyword, ends with a
  reason to click ("— with real prices from our UK range").
- **Cover image**: real product/lifestyle photo, never stock-looking.

## Step 5 — After publishing (the part everyone skips)

1. **Google Search Console → URL Inspection → Request Indexing** for the new
   URL. Without this, discovery can take weeks; with it, usually days.
2. After 3–4 weeks, check Search Console → Performance → filter by the post's
   URL. Look for queries ranking positions 5–15 with impressions: those are
   one content-tweak from page one — add an H2 answering that exact query.
3. Refresh the post every 6 months (update prices, change the year in the
   title). Google rewards maintained content; stale "2024" titles bleed clicks.

## Step 6 — Cadence

One genuinely good post a fortnight beats four rushed ones a week. The
priority order for the next posts, by buying intent and winnability:

1. How much does an outdoor sauna cost in the UK? ✅ (this pack)
2. Do you need planning permission for a garden sauna in the UK?
3. Infrared vs traditional sauna: which is right for you?
4. How much does a sauna cost to run in the UK? (electricity, deep-dive)
5. Cold plunge temperature: a beginner's guide
6. Barrel sauna vs cabin sauna: honest pros and cons
7. Sauna base & site preparation: what to do before delivery day
8. How long do outdoor saunas last? (materials & maintenance)

Each of these matches a real "People also ask" cluster, links naturally into
the catalogue, and builds the same topical territory: _the UK authority on
buying and owning outdoor wellness equipment._
